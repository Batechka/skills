# Typographic layout principles

This reference is a compact interpretation of public teaching material from Artem Gorbunov's *Typography and layout*. It is a decision aid, not a copy of the book or a Bureau style guide.

## Meaning before decoration

The page should communicate an ordered message. First identify what the reader should notice, understand, and do. Geometry, typography, imagery, and controls support that order.

## Inner and outer

Spacing signals relationship. Elements inside a coherent unit normally have a closer and more regular relationship than separate units. Compare neighboring gaps rather than choosing values one by one; a gap should explain whether elements belong together or merely align nearby.

## Modules and anchors

Modules organize repeated or related information. A module may be text, an image with caption, a control group, or a compact information block. Anchor objects give a composition stability: a large headline, leading image, key number, primary control, or navigation can establish the page's visual coordinate system.

## Type as geometry

Headlines tend to act as lines that set direction and emphasis. Body text acts as a rectangle that must remain comfortable to read. Tune measure, leading, and paragraph rhythm to the actual face and language. Do not use size alone to create hierarchy; combine type, space, position, and tone.

## Content connections

Captions must visibly belong to their images; labels to their controls; data labels to their values. Use placement and alignment before adding visual connectors. When several elements compete, make their roles explicit instead of giving each equal prominence.

## Responsive adaptation

Responsive design changes rules, not merely dimensions. Preserve reading sequence, semantic relationships, and usable controls at every viewport. Check long words and strings, narrow columns, image crops, tables, and touch interaction.
